Every note on this site started the same way: class happening, phone photo of the board, scattered PDFs on the class group. My workflow turns that mess into a single clean page per unit. Publishing the result is the discipline that keeps it honest.

## The pipeline

1. **Capture** — rough sketch in a notebook during class; keywords, formulas, nothing pretty.
2. **Same-day review** — within 24 hours I rewrite it as Markdown: what's the idea, what's the formula, what's the exam angle.
3. **Compress** — every unit gets a "takeaway" line. If I can't write the unit's one-sentence essence, I haven't understood it.
4. **Publish** — onto this site, subject by subject.

## Why Markdown

It's plain text, which means: no formatting fights, version control works, it renders on every device, and search can read it. My notes survive any future tool change because they're just text files.

## Why publish

This is the part that surprised me. Publishing forces me to:

- Explain in *complete sentences*, not bullet stubs
- Fix the shakier claims before they embarrass me publicly
- Keep the site as a genuine study tool, not a vanity page

When a real stranger reads a note and then passes the exam, the note just did its job.

## Steal what works

You don't need my exact format. The load-bearing ideas: capture on the day, convert to plain text, compress to an essence, and dare to share it. That last one — sharing — is 80% of the learning gain.
