The first semester at Pulchowk Campus is equal parts hype and grind. Here's my honest record of it — what the subjects feel like, where the week actually goes, and the specific mistakes I made so you can dodge them.

## The subjects meant business

Mathematics I, Engineering Physics and Chemistry in the first half; drawing and basic electronics on the applied side. The pace is fast and the lectures pile up — a day of solid mechanics can leave you a lecture behind in physics if you don't review that evening.

What surprised me most: **the labs.** The electronics and physics labs don't follow the lecture rhythm; they have their own tempo and their own deadlines. Budgeting for them day-by-day beats treating them as "easy."

## Lessons that would have saved me weeks

1. **Do tutorial problems from memory.** Reading a solved example feels like understanding; redoing it the same night shows you what you actually missed. This single change fixed my math marks.
2. **Take notes short, every day.** Handwriting a page of key points after each class beats re-copying full notes the night before the exam.
3. **The library is underrated.** Campus is busy, the library is calm, and past exam papers live on the department boards nearby.

## The honest part

The first month is disorienting: new hostels/accommodation, a new city rhythm, names and subjects all at once. Give yourself the grace to be average for a few weeks while you build the routine. It genuinely gets more manageable — but only if the routine is built day by day, not at the eleventh hour.
