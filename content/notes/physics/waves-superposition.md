# Wave Motion & Superposition

## Travelling waves

A disturbance travelling through a medium. A harmonic wave moving along +x:

```
y(x,t) = A sin(kx − ωt)
```

- k = 2π/λ (wave number)
- ω = 2πf
- speed v = fλ = ω/k

Waves transport **energy and momentum**, not the medium itself.

## Superposition principle

When two waves overlap, the net displacement is the SUM of the individual displacements at every point. Linear waves obey this exactly.

### Constructive ↔ destructive interference

For two waves in phase: amplitudes add → constructive.
For two waves π out of phase: amplitudes cancel → destructive.

### Standing waves

Two identical waves travelling in opposite directions:

```
y = 2A sin(kx) · cos(ωt)
```

The "sin(kx)" is a stationary envelope — **nodes** (zero displacement) and **antinodes** (max displacement) stay fixed.

Standing waves explain:

- Fixed strings (instrument strings, piano wires)
- Organ pipes and columns of air
- Microwave oven hot spots and dead zones

## Interference in thin films (why lens coatings exist)

A film of thickness t with refractive index n gives a path difference 2nt (normal incidence). Reflected rays interfere:

- Bright when path difference = mλ
- Dark when path difference = (m + ½)λ

That's exactly why camera lenses have **anti-reflection coatings** — a ¼-wave thick film makes reflected light cancel, sending energy into transmission instead.
