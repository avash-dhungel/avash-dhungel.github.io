# Introduction to Surveying

## Definition and objectives

Surveying is the art and science of **determining the relative positions** of points on or near the Earth's surface, and of using that data to produce maps or set out designed features.

Primary objectives:

- Measure distances, angles and heights
- Establish ground control points
- Plot results on a suitable scale
- Set out structures on the ground from plans

## Classification

| Classification | Kinds |
| --- | --- |
| By purpose | Geological, mining, military, engineering, cadastral |
| By instrument | Chain, compass, theodolite, plane-table, total station |
| By method | Triangulation, traversing, levelling |
| By nature | Plane (small areas) vs geodetic (large areas, curvature considered) |

For engineering works, **plane surveying** is usually valid — the area is small enough that the Earth's curvature is negligible (limit: roughly < 250 km²).

## Fundamental principles

1. **Work from whole to the part**: fix a few large control points first, then partition the area. Errors stay localized instead of accumulating.
2. **Locate a point by at least two independent measurements**: e.g., two distances, or a distance and an angle.

> These two principles appear in every method — chain, compass, theodolite, total station. Everything else is instrumentation.
