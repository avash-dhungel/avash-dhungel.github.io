# Unit 1 — Introduction to Engineering Geology

## What engineering geology is

Engineering geology applies geological knowledge to **planning, design and construction** of civil engineering works. It answers practical questions like:

- Where is safe ground to build a foundation?
- What is a tunnel's surrounding rock actually made of?
- Will a slope fail after a rainy week?

It sits at the overlap of geology and civil engineering — knowing the ground before we trust it.

## Why it matters in civil engineering

| Concern | Question it answers |
| --- | --- |
| Foundation design | Is this soil/rock strong enough to carry the load? |
| Tunnels | Will the rock stand without collapse? |
| Dams & reservoirs | Will water leak through the foundation? |
| Landslides | Is the slope stable, short-term and long-term? |

Ignoring geology leads to the classic failures — settling foundations, leaking reservoirs, and slipping slopes.

## The rock cycle (quick map)

- **Igneous** — formed from cooling magma/lava.
- **Sedimentary** — formed by compaction of weathered fragments.
- **Metamorphic** — igneous or sedimentary rock changed by heat and pressure.

Weathering breaks rock -> sediments -> compaction makes sedimentary rock -> deeper heat makes metamorphic rock — and the cycle loops through again.

> Takeaway for this unit: every map of hazards or material is a story of the rock cycle underfoot.
