# Electrochemistry & the Nernst Equation

## Electrochemical cells

A cell converts chemical energy to electrical energy (galvanic) or uses electricity to drive a reaction (electrolytic).

**Half-cells**: each electrode sits in its own electrolyte; oxidation happens at the **anode**, reduction at the **cathode**. Standard electrode potentials (E°) compared from the electrochemical series decide voltage.

**Daniel cell**:
- Anode (Zn → Zn²⁺ + 2e⁻): E° = −0.76 V
- Cathode (Cu²⁺ + 2e⁻ → Cu): E° = +0.34 V
- Cell EMF = E°cathode − E°anode = 0.34 − (−0.76) = **1.10 V**

## The Nernst equation

Cell potential depends on concentration and temperature:

```
E = E° − (0.0591/n) · log₁₀ Q     (at 25°C)
```

Where Q is the reaction quotient, n the number of electrons transferred. At equilibrium, E = 0 and Q = K (equilibrium constant) — the bridge between thermodynamics and electrochemistry.

## Corrosion: the electrochemical view

Most corrosion (rust) is a set of microscopic galvanic cells:

- Anode: iron → Fe²⁺ + 2e⁻ (metal dissolves)
- Cathode: O₂ + 2H₂O + 4e⁻ → 4OH⁻ (reduction)

Result: Fe²⁺ + OH⁻ → Fe(OH)₂ → rust.

### Prevention

- **Cathodic protection**: sacrificial anode (zinc on ships) or impressed current
- **Coating**: paint, galvanizing (zinc plated), anodizing
- **Alloying**: stainless steel keeps a passive chromium oxide film
- **Environmental control**: dry storage, inhibitors

> Read the Nernst equation as "concentration drives voltage". Nearly every exam question on this chapter is the Daniel cell + Nernst + a corrosion-prevention essay.
