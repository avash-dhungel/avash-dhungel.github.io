# Water Treatment & Hardness

## Hard and soft water

Hard water contains dissolved calcium and magnesium salts. It wastes soap, scales boilers and pipes, and complicates industrial processes.

| Type | Ions | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Temporary hardness | Ca(HCO₃)₂, Mg(HCO₃)₂ | Removed by boiling |
| Permanent hardness | CaSO₄, MgCl₂, … | Requires chemical treatment |

Total hardness = temporary + permanent.

## Measuring hardness

Hardness in mg/L as CaCO₃ equivalent. The EDTA complexometric titration is standard: EDTA chelates Ca²⁺/Mg²⁺; Eriochrome Black T changes from wine-red to blue at the endpoint.

```
Hardness = (V_EDTA × M × 100 × 1000) / V_sample   (mg/L as CaCO₃)
```

## Removal methods

1. **Lime–soda process** (cold/hot): precipitates Ca²⁺ and Mg²⁺ as CaCO₃ and Mg(OH)₂.
2. **Ion exchange / zeolite**: Na-zeolite swaps Na⁺ for Ca²⁺/Mg²⁺.
3. **Demineralization**: cation + anion exchangers give near-pure water.
4. **Boiling / distillation**: only temporary hardness.

## Why it matters to engineers

- **Boiler feed water**: hard water forms scale (CaCO₃) → bad heat transfer, tube failure, boiler corrosion.
- **Cooling systems**: scaling cuts efficiency and overheats equipment.
- **Concrete / construction**: water chemistry affects curing and reinforcement corrosion.

> Layered exam pattern: define hardness → distinguish temporary/permanent → choose the removal method → link scaling to boiler damage. Keep a table of ions and you hold the whole chapter.
